Simulator Information
Core Diffraction Theory
Born, M. & Wolf, E.
Principles of Optics: Electromagnetic Theory of Propagation, Interference and Diffraction of Light
Cambridge University Press, 7th Edition (1999) — §8.5 Fraunhofer Diffraction, §8.6 Gratings
The definitive reference for wave optics. Covers single-slit, double-slit, and N-slit diffraction patterns rigorously using scalar diffraction theory. All intensity formulas in this simulator derive from §8.5–8.6.
I(y) = I₀ · sinc²(πay/λL) · [sin(Nπdy/λL) / (N·sin(πdy/λL))]²
General N-slit diffraction intensity (Born & Wolf §8.6.1)
DOI: 10.1017/CBO9781139644181 Cambridge Press
Goodman, J.W.
Introduction to Fourier Optics
Roberts & Company, 3rd Edition (2005) — §4.4 Fraunhofer Diffraction
Essential treatment of diffraction using Fourier transform methods. The far-field (Fraunhofer) diffraction pattern is the Fourier transform of the aperture function — directly implemented in the Fourier tab.
U(x,y) = (e^{ikz}/iλz) ∫∫ U₀(ξ,η) e^{-i2π(xξ + yη)/λz} dξdη
Fraunhofer diffraction integral (Goodman §4.2)
Publisher Page Wikipedia: Fourier Optics
Young, T.
The Bakerian Lecture: On the Theory of Light and Colours
Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London, 92, 12–48 (1802)
The original double-slit experiment demonstrating the wave nature of light. Young's interference fringes (d·sin θ = mλ) form the foundation for all interference-based imaging systems.
Δy = λL / d
Fringe spacing in the small-angle approximation (sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ y/L)
DOI: 10.1098/rstl.1802.0004 Royal Society
Rayleigh, Lord (Strutt, J.W.)
Investigations in Optics, with Special Reference to the Spectroscope
Philosophical Magazine, Series 5, 8(49), 261–274 (1879)
Establishes the Rayleigh criterion for angular resolution of optical instruments. Two points are just resolved when the central maximum of one falls on the first minimum of the other.
θ_R = 1.22 λ / D
Rayleigh criterion for a circular aperture of diameter D
DOI Link Wikipedia
Abbe, E.
Beiträge zur Theorie des Mikroskops und der mikroskopischen Wahrnehmung
Archiv für Mikroskopische Anatomie, 9, 413–468 (1873)
Derives the fundamental resolution limit of optical microscopes. The minimum resolvable feature size is d = λ/(2·NA), where NA is the numerical aperture. Directly applied in the Fluorescence Microscope application.
d_min = λ / (2 · NA)
Abbe diffraction limit (NA = n·sin θ_max)
Wikipedia: Abbe Limit Zeiss: Resolution
Ghost Imaging & Quantum Correlations
Pittman, T.B., Shih, Y.H., Strekalov, D.V. & Sergienko, A.V.
Optical imaging by means of two-photon quantum entanglement
Physical Review A, 52(5), R3429–R3432 (1995)
The original ghost imaging experiment using entangled photon pairs from spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC). The signal photon never interacts with the object — only the idler is detected in coincidence.
G⁽²⁾(r₁,r₂) = ⟨Ê⁻(r₁)Ê⁻(r₂)Ê⁺(r₂)Ê⁺(r₁)⟩
Second-order quantum coherence function (Glauber 1963)
DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevA.52.R3429 arXiv Preprint
Shapiro, J.H.
Computational ghost imaging
Physical Review A, 78(6), 061802(R) (2008)
Shows that ghost imaging does not require quantum entanglement — classical thermal light with computed speckle patterns works. This is the algorithm implemented in this simulator's ghost imaging engine.
G(x,y) = ⟨B·P(x,y)⟩ − ⟨B⟩·⟨P(x,y)⟩
Computational ghost imaging reconstruction (covariance method)
DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevA.78.061802 arXiv: 0807.2614
Bromberg, Y., Katz, O. & Silberberg, Y.
Ghost imaging with a single detector
Physical Review A, 79(5), 053840 (2009)
Demonstrates ghost imaging using a single-pixel (bucket) detector. The reconstruction improves as G(x,y) → O(x,y) with increasing number of speckle patterns N, with SNR ∝ √N.
SNR ∝ √N_patterns · (signal/noise_per_pattern)
Ghost imaging signal-to-noise ratio scaling
DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevA.79.053840 arXiv: 0902.3634
Gong, W. et al.
Three-dimensional ghost imaging lidar via sparsity constraint
Scientific Reports, 6, 26133 (2016)
Extension of ghost imaging to 3D LIDAR through scattering media (fog/smoke). Demonstrates penetration of turbid media using NIR ghost imaging, directly corresponding to the Fog Imaging application.
DOI: 10.1038/srep26133 Nature/Sci.Reports
Quantum Optics & Entanglement
Gerry, C. & Knight, P.
Introductory Quantum Optics
Cambridge University Press (2005) — §7.3 Entangled States, §9 Quantum Information
Comprehensive treatment of quantum optical states including Bell states, SPDC, and Hong-Ou-Mandel effect. The Bell state visualization in the Entangled tab is based on the Bloch sphere representation of §3.4.
|ψ⁻⟩ = (1/√2)(|HV⟩ − |VH⟩)
Singlet Bell state (one of four maximally entangled states)
DOI: CBO9780511791239 Wikipedia: Bell States
Glauber, R.J.
The Quantum Theory of Optical Coherence
Physical Review, 130(6), 2529–2539 (1963) — Nobel Prize in Physics 2005
Establishes the quantum theory of optical coherence. Defines the nth-order coherence functions G⁽ⁿ⁾ used throughout quantum imaging. The G⁽²⁾ function directly connects to HBT experiments and ghost imaging.
DOI: 10.1103/PhysRev.130.2529 Nobel Lecture
Applied Diffraction & Instrumentation
Bragg, W.L. & Bragg, W.H.
The Reflection of X-Rays by Crystals
Proceedings of the Royal Society A, 88(605), 428–438 (1913) — Nobel Prize 1915
Derives Bragg's Law for X-ray diffraction from crystal planes. Crystal planes with spacing d diffract X-rays constructively when 2d·sin θ = nλ. Applied in the X-Ray Crystal Diffraction application (N-grating analog).
2d · sin(θ) = n · λ
Bragg's Law for constructive interference from crystal planes
DOI: 10.1098/rspa.1913.0040 Wikipedia
Ntziachristos, V.
Going deeper than microscopy: the optical imaging frontier in biology
Nature Methods, 7(8), 603–614 (2010)
Reviews optical imaging techniques for biological tissue. NIR (700–1000nm) reduces Mie scattering, enabling deeper penetration — the physical basis for the Medical Low-Light application preset.
DOI: 10.1038/nmeth.1483 Nature Methods
Moreira, A. et al.
A Tutorial on Synthetic Aperture Radar
IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Magazine, 1(1), 6–43 (2013)
Tutorial on SAR and optical remote sensing from space. Resolution formula Δr = 1.22λH/D used in the Remote Sensing (Satellite) application. LIDAR at 1550nm is eye-safe per IEC 60825-1.
DOI: 10.1109/MGRS.2013.2248301
